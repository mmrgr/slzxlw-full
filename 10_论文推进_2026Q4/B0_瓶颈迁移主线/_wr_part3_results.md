## 3. Results

### 3.1 Where the system binds, and how the binding point migrates

The two endpoints fail in different places, and the ordering of their failures carries the argument. At the water-scarce endpoint the first constraint to fail on a daily step is source abstraction, at 1200 MW, with treatment-works utilisation failing at the same point; grid connection does not fail until 1450 MW. At the cool endpoint the order is reversed: grid connection fails first at 1500 MW and source abstraction is not reached until 1800 MW. The model skeleton, the constraint set and the scan protocol are identical at the two endpoints; only the climate driver and the facility parameters differ. The difference in starting point must therefore be attributed to residual-margin structure rather than to facility scale.

**Table 2.** First-failure capacity of each constraint, daily step, joint constraint set (MW).

| Constraint | D-HA | D-CO |
|---|---:|---:|
| Maximum source abstraction ratio | **1200** | 1800 |
| Maximum WTW utilisation | **1200** | 1600 |
| Reclaimed substitution ratio (policy) | 1350 | never |
| Maximum daily-average grid connection ratio | 1450 | **1500** |
| Maximum local connection ratio | 1550 | 1600 |
| Peak capacity ratio | 1750 | never |
| Domestic unmet demand | 1850 | never |
| Maximum WWTW utilisation | never | never |
| System reliability fraction | never | never |
| Cooling water unmet | never | never |

The migration sequence differs in more than its starting point. The water-scarce endpoint passes through six distinct failure capacities spanning 650 MW, beginning on the water side, moving to the grid in the middle range, and ending at peak capacity and domestic supply. The cool endpoint passes through three distinct failure capacities spanning 300 MW, beginning at the grid, then moving to local connection and treatment, and reaching source abstraction only at the end. Because the two endpoints share everything except climate and facility parameters, this divergence in starting point, length and ordering is what a residual-margin explanation predicts and what a scale explanation cannot produce.

Six of the ten constraints never carry information in the scanned range at the cool endpoint, and three at the water-scarce endpoint. A non-informative constraint is not evidence that the system is safe; it means the scan never reached it, and we report it as such. Two of these negative results are substantive. Wastewater treatment utilisation never fails at either endpoint, peaking at 0.892 and 0.860 against a threshold of 0.95, so under this parameter set wastewater capacity is not a bottleneck, contrary to the common intuition that data-centre water demand overwhelms sewerage. Domestic unmet demand is identically zero and never informative at the cool endpoint, which is why we make no claim about domestic supply consequences there at all.

Interventions confirm that the binding constraint, not the intervention label, determines whether an investment has any effect. Applying nine one-to-one engineering interventions to the water-scarce endpoint, only leakage reduction of 30% yields a gain, raising capacity from 1150 to 1300 MW, and it does so by relaxing the water side. Expanding grid connection by 50% at that endpoint yields exactly zero, at a median investment of 44,921 ten-thousand yuan. At the cool endpoint the pattern inverts: expanding grid connection yields 100 MW, from 1450 to 1550, while leakage reduction yields zero at a median investment of 2718.8 ten-thousand yuan. Cross-application is ineffective in both directions. Zero gain is therefore not zero cost, and that combination is the most directly planning-relevant result in this section. The unit costs of the two effective interventions differ by a factor of 32.5, but because only one of the two cost inputs has an official source we read that ratio as an order-of-magnitude comparison and draw no general conclusion from it.

### 3.2 A closed-form threshold on the substitution ratio

Everything above was obtained at a fixed substitution target, 0.70 at the water-scarce endpoint and 0.45 at the cool endpoint, treated as an attribute of the city. It is in fact a policy variable. A city may choose to raise its reclaimed target from 0.40 to 0.70, and the question is whether that choice relocates the carrying capacity. The intuitive answer is that it cannot, because the substitution ratio is only a source-mix and total source volume is conserved. We show that this intuition holds below a threshold and fails above it.

Consider a cooling tower in steady state, neglecting drift, with evaporation $E$, blowdown $B$, make-up $M$ and cycles of concentration $\mathrm{CoC}=M/B$. Salts do not evaporate, so $M\,C_{\mathrm{mix}}=B\,C_{\mathrm{loop}}$, and substituting the definition of CoC gives $C_{\mathrm{loop}}=\mathrm{CoC}\,C_{\mathrm{mix}}$. The loop is constrained by a scaling and corrosion limit $C_{\mathrm{loop}}\le L$, hence $\mathrm{CoC}\le L/C_{\mathrm{mix}}$. Make-up is a blend of reclaimed and potable water at ratio $r$, so $C_{\mathrm{mix}}(r)=rC_r+(1-r)C_p$. The model takes the tighter of the design and water-quality bounds,

$$\mathrm{CoC}(r)=\min\left(\mathrm{CoC}_d,\ \frac{L}{C_{\mathrm{mix}}(r)}\right).$$

Setting the water-quality bound equal to the design value gives the critical substitution ratio:

$$r^{*}=\frac{L/\mathrm{CoC}_d-C_p}{C_r-C_p}.$$

With endpoint parameters ($L=2200$ mg L$^{-1}$, $\mathrm{CoC}_d=5.0$, $C_p=180$ mg L$^{-1}$, $C_r=650$ mg L$^{-1}$) this gives $r^{*}=(440-180)/470=0.5532$. Because the model tracks two species, we also computed the chloride threshold, $(500/5-35)/(130-35)=0.6842$, and took the tighter of the two. TDS is the binding species, so the reported value needs no correction; and because the parameter set configures exactly these two species, this tightening is complete rather than partial. The general form is $r^{*}=\min_s[(L_s/\mathrm{CoC}_d-C_{p,s})/(C_{r,s}-C_{p,s})]$.

The two regimes behave qualitatively differently. For $r\le r^{*}$ the cycles of concentration equal the design value, make-up demand is constant, and the policy effect is a pure gain: potable abstraction falls by the substituted volume and total withdrawal does not move. For $r>r^{*}$ the cycles of concentration fall with $r$, make-up demand per unit of heat rejected rises through $M=E\,\mathrm{CoC}/(\mathrm{CoC}-1)$, and the gain is partly eroded. The make-up penalty relative to the constant-CoC baseline is $+1.27\%$ at $r=0.60$, $+4.08\%$ at $r=0.70$, $+7.06\%$ at $r=0.80$ and $+10.21\%$ at $r=0.90$.

Because $r^{*}$ is easily mistaken for a city constant, it is worth noting that its sign and magnitude are themselves classificatory. Writing $C^{*}\equiv L/\mathrm{CoC}_d$ for the highest make-up salinity tolerable at the design cycles, four regimes follow (Table 3). Regime I, $C^{*}\le C_p$, means the tolerable make-up salinity is below that of potable water itself: any substitution carries a cost and no pure-gain interval exists. Regime II, $C_p<C^{*}<C_r$, is the only regime with a genuine policy trade-off, and it contains both of our endpoints. Regime III, $C^{*}\ge C_r$, means substitution is never costly. Regime IV, $C_r\le C_p$, means reclaimed water is cleaner than potable water and the problem disappears.

**Table 3.** Four regimes defined by the position of $C^{*}=L/\mathrm{CoC}_d$ relative to the two source salinities.

| Regime | Criterion | Position of $C^{*}$ | Policy meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | $r^{*}\le0$ | $C^{*}\le C_p$ | Every substitution costs; no pure-gain interval |
| II | $0<r^{*}<1$ | $C_p<C^{*}<C_r$ | A real threshold exists; the only trade-off regime |
| III | $r^{*}\ge1$ | $C^{*}\ge C_r$ | Substitution is never costly |
| IV | — | $C_r\le C_p$ | Reclaimed water is cleaner; problem disappears |

Both endpoints fall in Regime II, since $C^{*}=2200/5=440$ lies between 180 and 650. Their difference is not two mechanisms but one mechanism under two policy targets: the water-scarce target of 0.70 exceeds $r^{*}$ while the cool target of 0.45 does not. We verified that the regimes are behaviourally distinct rather than merely algebraically distinct by holding the endpoint at 800 MW and varying only $\mathrm{CoC}_d$. The nominal case ($\mathrm{CoC}_d=5.0$, $r^{*}=0.5532$) is flat up to the threshold and rises $6.48\%$ from $r=0$ to $r=0.8$. Regime I ($\mathrm{CoC}_d=15.0$, $r^{*}=-0.0709$) starts with a CoC of 12.2222 rather than the design value of 15, which is the defining signature of a design value that is unreachable because the quality constraint binds from the outset; it is already $4.43\%$ above baseline at $r=0.2$ and $20.74\%$ by $r=0.8$. Regime III ($\mathrm{CoC}_d=2.5$, $r^{*}=1.4894$) holds CoC at 2.5 across all five sampled points and shows exactly $0.00\times10^{0}\%$ change. The high-$r$ behaviour carries a diagnostic: Regimes I and II coincide exactly once the quality bound falls below both design values, total withdrawal matching to the digit at $r=0.8$ (23,840.93 ML), whereas Regime III never merges because its design value stays under the quality bound.

The threshold's form is structural and its value is conditional. Elasticities at the nominal point are $+1.6923$ for $L$, $-1.6921$ for $\mathrm{CoC}_d$, $-1.3828$ for $C_r$ and $-0.3093$ for $C_p$; the near-equality of the first two reflects that both enter only through $C^{*}$. A 10% change in either moves $r^{*}$ by roughly 0.05 to 0.09. Across a full-factorial grid of 320 parameter combinations, Regime II occupies 48.8% of the space, Regime I 25.3%, Regime III 19.7% and Regime IV 6.2%, with $r^{*}$ inside Regime II ranging from 0.0196 to 0.9167. The existence of a threshold-typed response is therefore robust, being fixed by the min structure of the CoC expression, whereas the numerical value of $r^{*}$ and the regime assignment of any particular city are not.

Three empirical checks support the derivation. First, the cycles of concentration are a response variable, not an input constant. Across 40 sampling points (two endpoints, five substitution ratios, four capacities) the maximum deviation between the model and the analytic expression is $8.882\times10^{-16}$, at machine precision; CoC falls from 5.0 at $r\le0.5$ to 3.648 at $r=0.9$, a decline of 27%. We record this because an earlier internal check scanned only the capacity dimension at the default ratio, observed CoC fixed at 4.322 and 5.000, and concluded that CoC was an input parameter. That conclusion was wrong: 4.322 is simply the value of $L/C_{\mathrm{mix}}$ at $r=0.70$.

Second, the penalty is measurable and independent of capacity. Raising $r$ from 0.5 to 0.8 raises total withdrawal by $+6.47\%$ at 200 MW and $+6.48\%$ at 800, 1200 and 1600 MW, a range of 0.012 percentage points. This invariance is the point: a penalty that arose from load growth would scale with capacity, whereas one that arises from blend chemistry does not. Attribution follows from back-calculating evaporation through $M=E\,\mathrm{CoC}/(\mathrm{CoC}-1)$ at the water-scarce endpoint and 1600 MW: evaporation is essentially constant (35,815.7 to 35,544.1 ML, $-0.76\%$) while total withdrawal rises $9.37\%$. The increment can only come from the water-quality term.

Third, the penalty contribution is exactly zero for $r\le r^{*}$. Defining it as actual withdrawal minus withdrawal at $r=0$ for the same capacity, we obtain $0.000\times10^{0}$ ML at the water-scarce endpoint and 1200 MW, and $7.276\times10^{-12}$ ML at the other three sampled combinations of endpoint and capacity, against 1257.9 to 3146.5 ML once $r\ge0.7$. A supporting falsification tests the obvious alternative explanation: at 1200 MW, raising $r$ from 0 to 0.5 raises reclaimed use from 0 to 16,790 ML, half of total withdrawal, while total withdrawal, the abstraction ratio and treatment utilisation do not move by a single digit. Raised reclaimed use by itself therefore does not load the system; only the blend chemistry does.

### 3.3 The bottleneck is an endogenous function of the policy variable

The results above concern total withdrawal. The carrying-capacity question is separate, and it can only be answered under the purely physical constraint set, because leaving the policy constraint in place makes the answer self-referential. Under that set the water-scarce endpoint's capacity is 1200 MW for $r\le0.5$, falls to 1150 MW at $r=0.6$ and 0.7, and to 1100 MW at $r=0.8$ and 0.9, with source abstraction as the first-failing constraint throughout. The cool endpoint's capacity stays at 1450 MW until $r=0.9$, where it falls to 1400 MW and the binding constraint becomes local connection. The headline effect is therefore about 100 MW at the water-scarce endpoint and essentially nothing at the cool endpoint.

Quantisation constrains what may be cited. Because the effect is roughly two grid steps and the first-failure boundary need not fall on a grid point, we recomputed at 25 MW and 10 MW. The zero-effect region is identical on all three grids, which is the strongest form of the result and the one our falsifiability claim rests on. At the endpoint ($r=0.9$) the decline is $-100$, $-100$ and $-90$ MW, a range of 10 MW, so the figure may be cited as approximately 100 MW with a $\pm25$ MW quantisation caveat. In the intermediate range ($r=0.6$ and $0.8$) the ranges are 30 to 40 MW and the precise declines must not be cited: the 50 MW grid reports $-50$ and $-100$ where the 25 MW grid reports $-25$ and $-75$ and the 10 MW grid reports $-10$ and $-70$. The cause is not instability of the effect but the subtraction of two rounding errors, since a true boundary of 1187 MW would be reported as 1150, 1175 and 1180 on the three grids. Absolute values also show a systematic dependence, approaching the upper bound monotonically as the grid is refined (1150, 1175, 1190 at $r=0.6$), so the 50 MW grid underestimates capacity by 0 to 25 MW. This is an inherent property of defining a maximum feasible point on a discrete grid, not a model error.

The confound control is the hardest evidence available, because it holds capacity, climate driver and city load fixed and varies only the blend. At the water-scarce endpoint and 1200 MW, the abstraction ratio is 0.9497 for $r\le0.5$, compliant against the 0.95 threshold, and 0.9513 at $r=0.6$, non-compliant. Raising the reclaimed target from 0.5 to 0.6 alone, with capacity, climate and load unchanged, therefore pushes a compliant city across the threshold. Treatment utilisation moves the same way, from 0.9463 to 0.9479 at $r=0.6$ and past 0.95 by $r=0.7$. Meanwhile the grid constraint stays at 0.8326 with a change of exactly 0.0000 across the entire range. The water-quality penalty strikes the water side and does not touch the electricity side. The same pattern reproduces independently at the cool endpoint and 1600 MW, where the abstraction ratio rises from 0.9306 to 0.9472 and treatment utilisation from 0.9503 to 0.9674 while the grid ratio remains at 1.0959.

The response is a step rather than a gradient, and the arithmetic shows why. Constraint margins at a carrying boundary are necessarily small by construction: the first-failing source abstraction ratio sits 0.00028 above threshold one grid step earlier at $r=0.5$ and 0.00543 and 0.00541 at $r=0.6$ and 0.8. The penalty increment, by contrast, is 0.013 to 0.017. The penalty therefore exceeds the boundary margin by a factor of 2 to 50, which is why crossing $r^{*}$ must push the boundary forward by at least one grid step. It also explains why a penalty percentage cannot be converted into a megawatt decline: the decline is fixed jointly by grid resolution and local margin, a threshold-crossing problem rather than a continuous response.

Why the water-scarce endpoint loses capacity while the cool endpoint does not requires a second condition. The penalty occurs at both endpoints, which share identical water-quality parameters and therefore the same $r^{*}=0.5532$. It changes carrying capacity only where the first-failing constraint is on the water side, which is true at the water-scarce endpoint and false at the cool endpoint, where the grid fails first. The mechanism is thus stated completely as two conditions: $r>r^{*}$ and a water-side bottleneck. It is not a general claim that water saving is harmful. The cool endpoint is the negative result that establishes the second condition, and we report it rather than setting it aside.

### 3.4 Operationalising the criterion: a two-number city screen

The criterion becomes usable when it is expressed in quantities that cities already publish. Let the city's total potable use be $P$ and its total reclaimed use be $R$. If data centres take a share $\alpha$ of city potable use and a share $\beta$ of city reclaimed use, then campus make-up is $D=\alpha P+\beta R$ and the campus substitution ratio is

$$r=\frac{\beta R}{\alpha P+\beta R}=\frac{1}{1+(\alpha/\beta)\,k},\qquad k\equiv\frac{P}{R}.$$

Setting $r=r^{*}$ gives the critical city ratio $k^{*}=(1/r^{*}-1)/(\alpha/\beta)$. With the shares documented for Santa Clara, California, of 6% of city potable water and 26% of city reclaimed water, $k^{*}=(1/0.5532-1)/(6/26)=3.50$. The screen then requires only two published numbers: a city whose ratio of potable to reclaimed use falls below 3.50 has a data-centre substitution ratio that necessarily exceeds $r^{*}$, and enters the water-quality penalty region. This is a transferable screening procedure, not an assessment of any one city; the single city below is the substitution that demonstrates it.

For Santa Clara the 2025 Urban Water Management Plan gives two calibers. On supply capacity (SFPUC 5041, Valley Water 4560, groundwater 23,048 and recycled 6017, totalling 38,666 acre-feet per year) potable is 32,649 and reclaimed 6017, giving $k=5.43$ and $r=0.444$, which is 10.9 percentage points below $r^{*}$. On 2025 actual use potable is 16,857 and reclaimed 3871, giving $k=4.35$ and $r=0.499$, which is 5.4 percentage points below. Both calibers agree that the threshold has not been crossed, but the actual-use margin is only 9.8%. An independent source corroborates the ratio: the California Electronic Annual Report for 2022 gives potable 16,913 and recycled 3450 acre-feet, hence $k=4.90$, differing from the plan by 0.3% on potable and 11% on recycled. A structural observation explains why the actual-use caliber sits closer to the threshold: the city as a whole is lightly loaded, using 20,728 of 38,666 acre-feet per year, or 53.6% of supply capacity, while its recycled subsystem is heavily loaded at 3871 of 6017, or 64.3%. When total water is abundant, what constrains the data-centre blend is the relative tightness of the recycled subsystem rather than total volume.

Applied across a state, the screen identifies a non-trivial population. Using the full 2022 California Electronic Annual Report, we extracted every public water system reporting both potable and recycled production and computed $k$, retaining systems with potable production of at least 100 acre-feet per year, $k\ge0.2$ and a recycled share no greater than 90%. That leaves 169 systems with a median $k$ of 13.54, of which 13.6% (23 systems) fall below $k^{*}=3.50$; among the 93 large systems serving 50,000 or more people the share is 8.6%. Roughly one recycled-water system in seven would cross the threshold if it hosted data centres with Santa Clara's 6%/26% shares. The conditional is essential: the shares are city-specific and we hold documented shares for one city only, so this distribution bounds the criterion's reach and does not assert that any of these cities has crossed. The sample is also selected, since California has more than 7,000 systems reporting recycled production but most are wholesalers that report no potable production and cannot yield a $k$.

Three limits on this section must travel with it. It is an application, not a verification: we have not observed make-up demand rising after a city's ratio crossed $r^{*}$, which would require facility-level time series that we do not hold. The 6% and 26% shares have different denominators and cannot be summed to 32%, and the reporting period behind them is undisclosed. And $r=0.499$ is a city-wide average over 56 data centres, not a property of any individual facility. Because $r^{*}$ carries roughly $\pm10\%$ parameter uncertainty, a true value near 0.51 would place Santa Clara at or beyond the threshold, so "not crossed" is a conclusion under nominal parameters rather than a robust one.
